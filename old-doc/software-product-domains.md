# Software Product Domains

A practical reference for exploring software product ideas across industries and business functions. Each domain includes example product types and the workflows they commonly support. This is a broad starting point, not an exhaustive or mutually exclusive classification.

## 3. Audience and Delivery Models

These describe how a product reaches its users. They are separate from its domain and function.

| Dimension | Common options |
| --- | --- |
| Customer or audience | Consumers (B2C), businesses (B2B), governments (B2G), internal employees, developers |
| Industry focus | Vertical: tailored to one industry; horizontal: usable across industries |
| User interface | Web application, mobile application, desktop application, command line, embedded interface |
| Integration interface | Application programming interface (API), software development kit (SDK), plug-in |
| Deployment | Hosted software as a service (SaaS), self-hosted, on-premises, hybrid, device or edge deployment |
| Revenue model | Subscription, usage charges, license purchase, transaction fees, advertising, paid services |

A product may combine several options. For example, a subscription service can include a web dashboard, a mobile app, and an API.

## 4. Combining Domains into Product Ideas

Start with **an industry, a specific user, and a recurring problem**, then select the supporting product functions.

| Product idea | Industry domain | Supporting categories | Primary user and problem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clinic appointment and follow-up platform | Healthcare | Scheduling, communication, payments | Clinic staff need to manage bookings and patient follow-ups |
| Construction purchasing and cost tracker | Construction | Procurement, accounting, project management | Site managers need to coordinate material orders and project costs |
| School administration portal | Education | Payments, communication, document management | Administrators need to coordinate enrollment, fees, and notices |
| Distributor ordering portal | Wholesale and distribution | Inventory, CRM, payments | Retail buyers need to check availability and place repeat orders |
| Equipment maintenance platform | Manufacturing | Asset management, IoT, analytics | Maintenance teams need to schedule service and track equipment condition |
| Volunteer and donation platform | Nonprofits | CRM, scheduling, payments | Coordinators need to manage supporters, contributions, and volunteer shifts |
| Local service booking marketplace | Home and local services | Marketplace operations, scheduling, payments | Customers and service providers need to arrange and track jobs |
| Software incident management platform | Technology operations | Monitoring, collaboration, automation | Engineering teams need to detect issues and coordinate recovery |

## 5. Questions for Defining a Product

For any selected domain, document:

1. **Target users:** Who uses the product, who buys it, and who administers it?
2. **Problem:** Which recurring task is difficult, expensive, slow, or error-prone?
3. **Core workflow:** What triggers the work, what steps follow, and what completes it?
4. **Core records:** Which entities must the product manage, such as customers, orders, appointments, or assets?
5. **Minimum viable product (MVP):** What is the smallest useful workflow that can be delivered end to end?
6. **Integrations:** Which existing systems, devices, or data sources must it connect to?
7. **Operating requirements:** What access controls, privacy, accessibility, availability, and domain-specific requirements apply?
8. **Business model:** Who pays, for what, and how often?
9. **Success measure:** What observable improvement would show that the product solves the problem?

Use the domain lists to explore possibilities, then narrow the product around one audience and one clearly defined workflow.
